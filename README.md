It is important to examine which factors and how they influenced people during Covid-19 pandemic to comply with the protective behaviour. This research investigated whether COVID-19 severity (case and death counts) improved the prediction of face mask wearing and general protective behaviour, whether this influence was different across Australian states and whether the determinants of these behaviours remained stable across pandemic waves (Early Pandemic, Delta, Omicron). Two publicly available datasets, the YouGov Behaviour Tracker and the Oxford COVID-19 Government Response Tracker (OxCGRT) were combined and used to train Random Forest and XGBoost models. Adding COVID-19 severity features marginally improved predictive performance (ROC AUC) compared to the models without those features, even though the case counts consistently ranked as a top determinant when included, suggesting that its influence was mediated through correlated temporal and geographic features such as week and state. The influence of COVID-19 severity on protective behaviour differed across states where stronger influence showed for face mask wearing than for general protective behaviour. Determinants of protective behaviour were moderately stable across pandemic waves while the importance of mandate status and COVID-19 severity shifted across waves. Overall, the findings implies that the government mandates and behavioural consistency had a stronger influence on protective health behaviours than COVID-19 severity. This can help public health authorities to aid for future pandemics across different regions and phases of future pandemics.
